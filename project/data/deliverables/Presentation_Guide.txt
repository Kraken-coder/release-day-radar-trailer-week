Slide 2  Question and measurement
Diptanshu  35 seconds
We make engagement precise by counting surviving top-level comments posted during the first seven elapsed days after one official original-language trailer. That entire window must finish before theatrical release. We compare this with three days of India nett collections, Friday through Sunday, including all languages. Every eligible film released on a Friday. Current views and likes were collected after release, so they cannot stand in for trailer-week views. We keep them for provenance but exclude them from the forecast. We count comments in every language and script, including mixed-language text. We do not infer a comment’s language from the film category or classify sentiment.

Slide 4  Data flow and cleaning
Diptanshu  45 seconds

We use films, trailers, daily collections and comments as four linked SQL tables, with seven example queries. Q&A preparation
Why comments rather than views? Current views are not archived first-week counts. Comment publication times let us reconstruct surviving events in the intended window.
Does 0.74 mean 74% accuracy? No. It is a rank correlation measuring association across films.
What does 32% mean? Relative reduction in mean absolute error, from 21.79 to 14.89 crore on the same 13 films.
Why keep duplicate text? Different comment IDs are separate posted events. Repetition is a bias warning, not proof of a bot.
Why not fill missing revenue with zero? Missing means unknown. We require all three opening days.
Why use IMDb? Only to demonstrate a large text-processing job. It does not add selected-film observations or sentiment predictions.
Why not announce extra screens? The study lacks local bookings, occupancy, screen economics and a validated prospective test.
Is language plus comments the best model? Not by MAE on this split. Comments alone is slightly better. All four models are disclosed.
